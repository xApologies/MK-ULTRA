# V4 RESEARCH NOTE — CONTINUITY AS AN OPEN FAMILY

The earlier temptation to divide continuity into two kinds is withdrawn as an exhaustive framing.

Let
\[
\mathfrak C(X,H,\Gamma,D,R,P,\ldots)
\]
denote the family of continuity relations exposed by the active question-landscape.

Current witnessed candidates include, without claim of exhaustion:

1. path continuity;
2. compositional continuity;
3. historical continuity;
4. identity continuity;
5. admissibility continuity;
6. domain continuity;
7. closure continuity;
8. provenance continuity;
9. witness continuity;
10. Resolution continuity;
11. readout continuity;
12. relational continuity;
13. structural continuity;
14. recursive continuity;
15. branch continuity;
16. orientation/chirality continuity;
17. information continuity;
18. causal continuity;
19. topological continuity in the strict sense;
20. continuity of a path-object under transformation.

The research problem is not to preserve this list as dogma. It is to determine whether these notions are independent, derived, ordered, generated by a smaller basis, related by implication, or members of a larger family not yet visible.

### Continuability

A useful distinction exposed by the expanded landscape is **continuability**. An admissible transition
\[
X_0\to X_1
\]
may terminate at a state for which
\[
\operatorname{Gen}(X_1)=\varnothing.
\]
The transition can satisfy several continuity relations while destroying the possibility of continued recursion. Thus continuity and continuability need not coincide.

### Higher path continuity

Once a path is itself promoted to an object, transformations between paths become available:
\[
\Gamma_0\Longrightarrow\Gamma_1.
\]
The question “are these the same path through refinement?” creates a higher-level continuity problem. Recursive promotion of paths to objects must remain bounded by the active closure discipline.
