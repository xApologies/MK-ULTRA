# THE QUESTION LANDSCAPE — Provenance

## Vera's methodological turn

A question is not treated here as an empty location into which an answer is inserted.

\[
oxed{	ext{A question provides the landscape.}}
\]

The question determines which distinctions become visible, which objects can be named, which transformations are relevant, what counts as evidence, and what an answer would even have to accomplish. An answer is therefore a location, path, region, obstruction, or boundary discovered within a landscape opened by inquiry.

The governing movement of this edition is

\[
	ext{question}	o
	ext{distinction}	o
	ext{definition}	o
	ext{formalization}	o
	ext{consequence}	o
	ext{better question}.
\]

Philosophy does not replace proof. It creates and interrogates the landscape in which proof has meaning.

## Landscape 1: What is an origin?

**Question.** What is an origin?

If every object is encountered after some chain of representation, what does it mean to call any point an origin rather than merely the earliest point we retained?

### Mathematical pressure

This question is productive only if it forces distinctions. We therefore ask which equivalence relations, witnesses, histories, projections, dependency cones, or closure conditions change when the question is posed this way rather than another way. The formal theory must not answer more than its premises license.

### Resolution test

Suppose two occurrences are identical under a coarse readout but distinct under a finer formal relation. The question is then not merely whether they are “the same,” but **at what Resolution, under which identity contract, and for which future questions** they are the same. This turns philosophical ambiguity into an explicit family of mathematical quotients.

### Open edge

A mature treatment preserves the unresolved remainder. If formalization answers the first question, the consequence is used to generate the next question rather than to declare the landscape exhausted.

# Interlude — The Number Two and the History of an Endpoint

Consider

\[
1+1=2,\qquad 3-1=2,\qquad 0+2=2.
\]

As arithmetic evaluations, all three expressions terminate at the same value. Under endpoint equivalence they coincide. But as decorated occurrences we may write

\[
(2,H_{1+1}),\qquad (2,H_{3-1}),\qquad (2,H_{0+2}).
\]

Whether these are “the same two” is not answered by arithmetic alone. It depends on what the active theory preserves.

If future admissibility depends on \(H\), then the carrier \(2\) is not a sufficient state. If no permitted observation or future transformation can distinguish the histories, a quotient may be justified. If the histories have been erased, their recovery cannot be manufactured from the endpoint alone.

This small example is a model for the entire library: the present can be extensionally equal while its becoming remains structurally distinct.

# Interlude — Past, History, Provenance, Memory

The following terms are deliberately separated:

- **past:** antecedent event structure, whether retained or not;
- **History:** ordered structure retained as relevant to the present occurrence;
- **provenance:** witnessed ancestry and admissible derivational origin;
- **memory:** information about antecedent structure currently accessible to the system.

These need not coincide. Their separation creates questions that can subsequently be formalized rather than hidden inside one overloaded word.
