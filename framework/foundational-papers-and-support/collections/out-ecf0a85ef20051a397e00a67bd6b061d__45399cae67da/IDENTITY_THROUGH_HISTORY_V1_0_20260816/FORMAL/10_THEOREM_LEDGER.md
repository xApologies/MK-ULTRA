# 10 — Theorem Ledger

## Recovered Transduction Geometry theorems

### T4 — Conditional Identity Persistence Under Presentation Variation
Identity persists when Bandwidth changes only operative availability, Resolution changes only readable equivalence classes, History remains in the same Global Realization Class, and declared Identity invariants are preserved.

### T5 — Closed Operative Loop Does Not Imply Identity Persistence
Readable/operative closure can coexist with nontrivial Identity Holonomy.

### T6 — Resolution-Relative Identity Invisibility
An Identity break can be invisible when the active Resolution quotient annihilates the distinguishing defect.

### T7 — Conditional Chirality-Holonomy-Induced Identity Breaking
Nontrivial chirality holonomy induces Identity breaking when Identity is sensitive to the defect and no compensation cancels it.

## New Atlas theorem A — Witness-Chain Persistence

If each segment of a composable History carries an Identity-preserving Witness and the witness class is closed under composition, the composed History carries an Identity-preserving Witness.

## New Atlas theorem B — Resolution Refinement at Fixed Ordering Address

For nested Resolution quotients at a fixed ordering label `o*`, the finer Resolution Domain canonically maps onto the coarser domain. Increased Resolution refines the readout; it does not create a new underlying History.

## New Atlas theorem C — Time-Shell Formal Identity Persistence

Let `T_s(t)=Lambda_chi(P_c(t),chi(t))`. If:

1. the underlying Global Realization Class is unchanged;
2. Identity Witnesses persist;
3. chirality transport is Identity-preserving or compensated;
4. `Lambda_chi` respects those witnesses;

then all `T_s(t)` belong to the same Formal Time-Shell Identity class even when Bandwidth, Resolution, or readable presentation varies.

## New Atlas proposition D — Persistent-History Temporal Readout

If persistent History classes carry a consistent partial order and `Ord` is order-preserving, then their local readouts admit a consistent readable temporal ordering.

This is a sufficient formal condition for operational temporal labeling, not a complete derivation of physical clock time.
