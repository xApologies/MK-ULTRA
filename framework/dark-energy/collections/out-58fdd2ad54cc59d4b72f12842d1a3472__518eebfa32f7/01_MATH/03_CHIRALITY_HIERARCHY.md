# Chirality Hierarchy

Canonical hierarchy:

\[
\boxed{
\text{tile}
\rightarrow
\text{seed}
\rightarrow
\text{chirality byte}
\rightarrow
\text{chirality block}
\rightarrow
\text{chirality fabric}.
}
\]

## Tile

One address/position in a declared Chirality domain; either realized or Void.

## Seed

A native \(2\times2\) four-address Chirality organization.

Canonical complementary occupancy presentation:

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}
\]

or its mirror, where `0` here is an occupancy indicator for the Void presentation, not an ordinary chromatic value.

## Chirality byte

Canonical spelling:

\[
\boxed{\text{chirality byte}}
\]

A native \(2\times2\times2\) eight-address local Chirality domain.

## Chirality block

A native shared

\[
\boxed{4\times4\times4}
\]

domain with

\[
|B|=64.
\]

Current half-occupancy working profile:

\[
|E|=32,\qquad |V|=32.
\]

The block may be organizationally decomposed as a \(2\times2\times2\) arrangement of chirality bytes without changing its native shared \(4^3\) geometry.

## Centerlessness

There is no privileged spatial center. A coordinate origin is a chart choice.

The whole block may carry a collective domain identity/readout without possessing a privileged central tile.
