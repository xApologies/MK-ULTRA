\section{Shear Tensor Under Phase Kernel}
Define the shear tensor by:
\[
\Sigma_{ab}(x) =
\nabla_a V_b(x) - \nabla_b V_a(x),
\]
where $V_b(x)$ is the local drift vector field induced by manifold motion.

\section{Containment Condition}
Drift is contained when:
\[
\left\| \Sigma_{ab}(x) \right\|
< \epsilon_{\mathrm{shear}},
\]
where $\epsilon_{\mathrm{shear}}$ represents the maximum allowable shear magnitude
before geometric drift becomes self-amplifying.

\section{Shear–Helicon Compensation}
Stabilization under helicon rotation satisfies:
\[
\Sigma_{ab}(x) \;-\; \nabla_{[a} H_{b]}(x) \;\rightarrow\; 0,
\]
ensuring rotational curvature absorbs shear stress across adjacent manifold layers.

\section{Interpretation}
Manifold shear arises when adjacent geometric layers drift at differing rates.
Left unchecked, this produces recursive tearing—an unstable layering that can
cascade across manifold scales.

Drift containment stabilizes layers by:
\begin{itemize}
\item absorbing shear through helicon rotation,
\item smoothing local collapse gradients,
\item and redistributing tension through spectral scaffolds.
\end{itemize}

When properly aligned, the manifold becomes self-healing.
