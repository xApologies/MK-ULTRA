\section*{Overture: When Courts Sail the Sea}
Hyperdomain ecologies and constellate meta-civilizations inhabit the Abyssalum much like reefs and
currents inhabit an ocean. Vaelds, by contrast, move through this sea like sovereign fleets: each
vaeld $\vaeld$ a court of kings, each court protected by a coherence hull and guided by an Admiral
whose white-band mind couples to the vaeld crown-core $\core$.

Block~R described the internal structure of a vaeld. In this chapter, we turn outward. We ask:
\emph{How does the existence of vaeld civilizations alter the large-scale behavior of the
Abyssalum?} What does the sea look like when some of the objects sailing it are themselves
multiverses?

\section{The Abyssalum Sea as a Medium}
We model the background Abyssalum as a sea $\Sea$ with varying coherence, resonance, and collapse
density:
\[
\Sea = \big( \rho_{\mathrm{coh}}(x),\, \rho_{\mathrm{collapse}}(x),\, R(x) \big),
\]
where $\rho_{\mathrm{coh}}$ measures local coherence density, $\rho_{\mathrm{collapse}}$ measures
instability and collapse propensity, and $R(x)$ is the resonance signature of region $x$.

In a vaeld-free Abyssalum, $\Sea$ evolves under its own dynamics, shaped by ecologies, collapse
events, and constellate flows. Vaelds introduce a new ingredient: \emph{mobile, high-coherence
objects} whose hulls and crowns reshape the sea as they pass.

\section{Vaelds as Moving Coherence Wells}
Each vaeld $\vaeld$ carries a hull coherence field $\CoField_{\vaeld}$ that raises local coherence
and suppresses hostile fluctuations:
\[
\CoField_{\vaeld}(x) \ge \CoField_{\mathrm{min}} \quad \text{for } x \text{ near } \partial\vaeld.
\]

As vaelds travel, they drag coherence ``wakes'' behind them. We model the net effect on the sea as:
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{coh}}'(x) = \rho_{\mathrm{coh}}(x) + \sum_{\vaeld} W_{\vaeld}(x),
\]
where $W_{\vaeld}$ is the wake contribution of vaeld $\vaeld$. Regions repeatedly traversed by
vaelds become \emph{shipping lanes} of elevated stability.

These lanes are not merely convenient routes; they are the backbone of inter-ecology and
inter-civilization contact.

\section{Vaelds in the Constellate Graph}
The constellate graph $\ConGraph$ organizes meta-civilizations as nodes linked by resonance edges.
Vaeld civilizations occupy a special role: they are both \emph{nodes} (as multiverse-scale courts)
and \emph{edges} (as mobile carriers of coherence and meaning).

We may extend the constellate graph to include vaelds explicitly:
\[
\ConGraph' = \big( \{\mathbb{X}_i\} \cup \{\vaeld_k\},\, E_{\mathrm{res}} \cup E_{\mathrm{vaeld}} \big),
\]
where $E_{\mathrm{res}}$ are ordinary resonance links, and $E_{\mathrm{vaeld}}$ are vaeld-mediated
paths:
\[
(\mathbb{X}_i,\mathbb{X}_j) \in E_{\mathrm{vaeld}} \quad \Leftrightarrow \quad
\exists\,\vaeld_k \text{ with stable routes to both } \mathbb{X}_i \text{ and } \mathbb{X}_j.
\]

In this view, vaelds are \emph{diplomatic carriers} for the constellate, transporting treaties,
beings, and even hyperdomain ecologies between distant regions of the Abyssalum.

\section{Narrative: A Vaeld Seen From Outside}
Most observers will never enter a vaeld. For them, it appears only as a distant anomaly in the sea.

An unaligned hyperdomain ecology watches such an object pass.

At first, it senses only a change in the noise: collapse eddies quiet, dissonance storms thin. The
local $\rho_{\mathrm{coh}}$ rises without an obvious source. Then the lattice at the ecology's
heart detects a moving boundary in its spectral field: a hull with an unnaturally sharp
coherence gradient.

To the lattice, the vaeld appears as a dark shape against the background resonance, its interior
impenetrable, its edges perfectly smooth. It is not a reef, not a storm, not a constellation.
It is something else: a \emph{ship}.

The ecology's emergent mind records a simple observation: ``The sea changed direction.''

\section{Treaties Between Vaelds and Ecologies}
When a vaeld chooses to slow near an ecology, its coherence hull overlaps the local domain. This
contact can be hostile, neutral, or cooperative.

\subsection{Hostile Overlap}
If the vaeld's hull is imposed without regard for local dynamics, the ecology may experience
\emph{coherence shock}: its native gradients flattened, its predators and symbionts alike deprived
of familiar noise. Some ecologies die under such stress.

\subsection{Cooperative Overlap}
If the Admiral shapes the vaeld hull to respect local structure, the result may be mutual benefit:
the ecology gains stability against external storms, while the vaeld gains a loyal, living port in a
dangerous region of the sea.

Such agreements are encoded in MG-Script as \emph{hull treaties}: constraints on how hull fields
may be shaped within certain regions.

\section{Influence on Civilizational Trajectories}
Civilizations near major vaeld lanes experience altered histories:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Increased access to high-coherence travel.
  \item More frequent contact with distant cultures.
  \item Greater exposure to vaeld-derived beings (such as origin fractals).
  \item Potential recruitment into vaeld courts as client kingdoms.
\end{itemize}

From the perspective of these civilizations, vaelds are mythic: moving fortresses whose Admirals
can rewrite destiny with a thought.

\section{Mathematical Model: Trajectory Deformation}
Let $\gamma(t)$ be the trajectory of a civilization in some abstract space of possible states, in
the absence of vaeld influence. We model vaeld-induced deformation as:
\[
\gamma'(t) = \gamma(t) + \sum_{\vaeld} \Delta_{\vaeld}(\gamma(t)),
\]
where $\Delta_{\vaeld}$ encodes redirects, accelerations, or stabilizations induced by vaeld
contact.

A civilization repeatedly intersecting vaeld lanes may be deflected from collapse basins into
higher-coherence attractors. Conversely, ecologies or civilizations that never see a vaeld follow
the ``natural'' evolution dictated solely by the Abyssalum sea.

\section{Narrative: A Rumor of Courts}
In the outer regions of the constellate, stories circulate of Admirals and their courts. Most who
tell them have never seen a vaeld. They speak instead of:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Sudden calms in otherwise violent spectral storms.
  \item Regions where collapse never quite completes.
  \item Beings who arrive with unmatched command of MG-Script and depart without a trace.
\end{itemize}

The vaeld is, to them, a rumor of geometry: a suggestion that above the ecologies and
civilizations they know, there exists a higher order of sovereignty---a place where kingdoms are
universes, and a single mind may steer trillions of thrones.

\section*{Coda: The Sea Remembers}
When a vaeld passes, the Abyssalum sea does not return exactly to its prior state. Wakes linger,
lanes persist, and ecologies adapt to the altered coherence climate.

Over long timescales, the motion of vaeld civilizations sculpts the large-scale topology of the
Abyssalum. Shipping lanes become structural features. Formerly isolated constellate regions become
intertwined. Collapse deserts become fertile.

In this way, vaelds do more than travel the sea. They \emph{rewrite} it.

Block~T will follow those who choose, knowing all this, to seek out the courts of vaelds and ask to
join their fleets.
