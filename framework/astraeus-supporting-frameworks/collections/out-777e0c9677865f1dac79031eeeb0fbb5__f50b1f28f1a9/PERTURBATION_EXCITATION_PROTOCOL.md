# Perturbation and Excitation Protocol

## Purpose

This protocol defines how perturbation is applied inside Pipeline 2 without altering the pipeline domains.

Perturbation is a diagnostic exposure, not a new operator.

## Perturbation Placement

Perturbations may be injected at:

- R3: proto-interaction awareness
- R4: corridor formation / elastic coupling
- R5: ordered topology resolution
- R6: constrained survivability stabilization
- R7: oscillatory persistence exposure

R0, R1, and R2 may be used as control regions, but Phase 4 perturbation primarily concerns R3 through R7.

## Perturbation Types

### Local Topology Stress
Localized bounded deformation of manifold values.

### Continuity Destabilization
Temporary disturbance of continuity distribution.

### Chirality Imbalance Readout Stress
Asymmetric read-access stress that does not write chirality.

### Corridor Stress
Stress applied to corridor candidate regions emitted by R4.

### Resolution Stress
Stress applied during R5 ordered topology resolution.

### Survivability Stress
Stress applied during R6 damping and recovery.

### Oscillatory Stress
Repeated R7 exposure.

## Response Classes

A perturbation response must be categorized as:

- damped
- recovered
- persistent
- amplified
- metastable
- resonant
- runaway
- destructive
- ancestry-preserving failure
- ancestry-losing failure

## Required Metrics

Every perturbation test must record:

- input topology class
- perturbation domain
- perturbation amplitude
- perturbation locality
- recovery correlation
- ancestry recovery score
- coherence score
- corridor strength delta
- chirality imbalance delta
- bandwidth occupancy delta
- R7 survivability score
- failure domain if any

## Prohibited Behavior

Perturbation tests must not:
- introduce new domains,
- change R4/R5/R6/R7 definitions,
- hardcode expected element outcomes,
- write chirality,
- overwrite ancestry.
