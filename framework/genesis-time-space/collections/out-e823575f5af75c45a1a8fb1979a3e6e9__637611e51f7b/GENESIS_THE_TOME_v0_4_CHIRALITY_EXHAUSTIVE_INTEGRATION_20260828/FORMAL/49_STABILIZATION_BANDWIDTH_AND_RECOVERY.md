# 49 — STABILIZATION, BANDWIDTH, AND RECOVERY

R22 tests whether stabilization alone can create the missing asymmetry.

A reverse-direction-only lock fails because alternate cycle routes restore recovery.

A stronger bounded lock can produce nontrivial state-dependent recoverability.

The source conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{stabilization lock}
+
\text{alternate-path topology}
+
\text{bounded transfer capacity}
\Rightarrow
\text{state-dependent recoverability}.
}
\]

## Bandwidth role

R23 identifies Bandwidth as **Available Organizational Freedom**.

Let \(\operatorname{Gen}_B(x)\) be primitive transformations that are both admissible and currently available.

Bandwidth may remove transformations. It may not invent new transformations or rewrite History.

For redistribution \(r:x\to y\), recovery under Bandwidth \(B\) is directed reachability from \(y\) back to the identity class of \(x\) in the Bandwidth-restricted History graph.

## Crucial distinction

\[
\boxed{\text{Bandwidth is a filter, not a silent preference rule.}}
\]

The existence of a Bandwidth restriction capable of breaking recoverability does not prove a canonical Bandwidth-update law.
