### 133.6 Emergence and reduction

An emergence claim and a reduction claim are distinct,

$$
X\xRightarrow{\mathrm{emerges}}Y\;\not\equiv\;Y\xRightarrow{\mathrm{reduces\ to}}X. \tag{133.6}
$$

Equation (133.6) is DETERMINISTIC_TYPE_NORMALIZATION. Each direction requires its own map, scale, conditions, and explanatory standard.

### 133.7 Representation boundary

$$

$$

Equation (133.7) is EVIDENCE_BOUNDARY. Model, object, and observation remain separately typed.

### 133.8 Analogy boundary

$$

$$

Equation (133.8) is EVIDENCE_BOUNDARY. Unmapped properties cannot be transferred silently.

### 133.9 Dimensional boundary

An embedding construction does not prove that extra dimensions are physical,

$$
\iota:X\hookrightarrow Y\;\not\Rightarrow\;Y\ \mathrm{is\ physically\ instantiated}. \tag{133.9}
$$

Equation (133.9) is EVIDENCE_BOUNDARY. Mathematical availability and ontology answer different questions.

### 133.10 Perception boundary

$$

Equation (133.10) is EVIDENCE_BOUNDARY. Measurement, physiology, context, and alternative causes must be distinguished.
