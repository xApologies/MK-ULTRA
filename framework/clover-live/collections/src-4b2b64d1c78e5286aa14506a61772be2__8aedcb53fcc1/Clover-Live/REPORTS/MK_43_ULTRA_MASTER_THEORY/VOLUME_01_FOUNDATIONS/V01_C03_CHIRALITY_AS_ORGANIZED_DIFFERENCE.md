### 3.2 Conventional mathematical representations

On an oriented \(n\)-dimensional vector space, a basis \((e_1,\ldots,e_n)\) and a transformed basis \((e'_1,\ldots,e'_n)\) have the same orientation when the transition determinant is positive. Orientation changes under

\[
\operatorname{sgn}\det A=-1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

A volume form \(\omega\) provides an intrinsic representation: orientation-preserving transformations retain the sign class of \(\omega\), whereas parity reversal changes it. Wedge products encode the antisymmetry on which this construction depends,

\[
\alpha\wedge\beta=(-1)^{pq}\beta\wedge\alpha,
\qquad
\alpha\in\Omega^p(M),\ \beta\in\Omega^q(M).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Equations (3.2)-(3.3) are `EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS`. Their handles - `@wedge_product`, differential forms, smooth maps, pushforwards, torsion, and volume structures - occur in the chapter assignment because they are conventional mathematical realizations against which the upstream claim must be tested.

In an even-dimensional spin representation, a chirality operator \(\Gamma\) satisfies

\[
\Gamma^2=I,
\qquad
\Gamma^\dagger=\Gamma,
\tag{3.4}

with projectors

\[
P_\pm=\frac{1}{2}(I\pm\Gamma),
\qquad
P_\pm^2=P_\pm,
\quad P_+P_-=0.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Equations (3.4)-(3.5) are also established mathematics. They provide one rigorous meaning of left and right chirality.
