# VOLUME IX - UNIVERSAL COUPLING

## Chapter 76 - Coupling Operators and Relations

### 76.1 Operators have domains

A linear coupling operator is a map

$$
C:D(C)\subseteq\mathcal H_X\longrightarrow\mathcal H_Y. \tag{76.1}
$$

Equation (76.1) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The domain is part of the operator; a symbolic rule without \(D(C)\) may fail to define a map on relevant states.

### 76.2 Boundedness

An operator is bounded when

$$
\|C\psi\|_Y\le M\|\psi\|_X
\quad\text{for all }\psi\in\mathcal H_X. \tag{76.2}
$$

Equation (76.2) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Bounded operators extend continuously to the full Hilbert space; many differential coupling operators do not.

### 76.3 Adjoint relation

The adjoint is defined by

$$
\langle C\psi,\phi\rangle_Y
=\langle\psi,C^\dagger\phi\rangle_X. \tag{76.3}
$$

Equation (76.3) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS on the appropriate domains. Adjointness depends on the inner products and boundary conditions.

### 76.4 Self-adjoint interaction

An interaction Hamiltonian must satisfy

$$
H_{\mathrm{int}}=H_{\mathrm{int}}^\dagger,
\qquad
D(H_{\mathrm{int}})=D(H_{\mathrm{int}}^\dagger) \tag{76.4}
$$

to generate unitary evolution. Equation (76.4) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Formal symmetry alone is insufficient.

### 76.5 Tensor-product coupling

A finite-rank interaction has the form

$$
H_{\mathrm{int}}
=\sum_{a=1}^r g_a A_a\otimes B_a. \tag{76.5}
$$

Equation (76.5) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The operator classes, common domain, convergence, and dimensions of \(g_a\) must be declared.

### 76.6 Integral-kernel coupling

Between function spaces, an operator may be represented as

$$
(C\psi)(y)
=\int_X K(y,x)\psi(x)\,d\mu_X(x). \tag{76.6}
$$

Equation (76.6) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The kernel, measures, integrability class, and boundary behavior determine whether the map exists and is bounded.

### 76.7 Composition relation

For \(A:X\to Y\) and \(B:Y\to Z\),

$$
B\circ A:X\longrightarrow Z. \tag{76.7}
$$

Equation (76.7) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. For unbounded operators, composition additionally requires \(A(x)\in D(B)\).

### 76.8 Commutator

The order sensitivity of two operators is measured by

$$
[A,B]=AB-BA. \tag{76.8}
$$

Equation (76.8) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Vanishing commutator can express compatible actions, but it is not the same relation as equality or identity.

### 76.9 Intertwining relation

An operator \(C\) intertwines representations \(U_X\) and \(U_Y\) when

$$
C\,U_X(g)=U_Y(g)\,C
\quad\text{for every }g. \tag{76.9}
$$

Equation (76.9) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. This is stronger than a graph claim that two objects are associated.

### 76.10 Projection relation

A projector obeys

$$
P^2=P. \tag{76.10}
$$

Equation (76.10) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. If \(P=P^\dagger\), it is an orthogonal projector; idempotence and self-adjointness are separate conditions.

### 76.11 Isometric embedding

An embedding \(V:\mathcal H_X\to\mathcal H_Y\) is isometric when

$$
V^\dagger V=I_X. \tag{76.11}
$$

Equation (76.11) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Surjectivity would additionally require \(VV^\dagger=I_Y\).

### 76.12 Effective operator

For a projection \(P\) and complementary projection \(Q=I-P\), a formal effective operator is

$$
H_{\mathrm{eff}}(z)
=PHP+PHQ\,(z-QHQ)^{-1}QHP. \tag{76.12}
$$

Equation (76.12) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS where the resolvent exists. It illustrates that eliminating a sector generally produces energy-dependent coupling.

### 76.13 Perturbative response

For \(H(g)=H_0+gV\), a nondegenerate eigenvalue satisfies at first order

$$
E_n(g)=E_n^{(0)}
+g\langle n|V|n\rangle+O(g^2). \tag{76.13}
$$

Equation (76.13) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS under standard perturbative hypotheses. It supplies a test of coupling strength rather than a universal law.

### 76.14 Coupled evolution

The propagator generated by a self-adjoint Hamiltonian is

$$
U(t)=e^{-itH/\hbar},
\qquad
U(t)^\dagger U(t)=I. \tag{76.14}
$$

Equation (76.14) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. An interaction relation must preserve the required probability structure or explicitly describe an open system.

### 76.15 Open-system coupling

A quantum channel \(\mathcal E\) has Kraus form

$$
\mathcal E(\rho)
=\sum_a K_a\rho K_a^\dagger,
\qquad
\sum_aK_a^\dagger K_a=I. \tag{76.15}
$$

Equation (76.15) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. A channel, Hamiltonian interaction, projector, and graph edge are different kinds of relation.

### 76.16 Relation typing

A formal relation record can be represented as

$$
R=(X,Y,\tau,\mathcal W), \tag{76.16}
$$

where \(\tau\) names the relation type and \(\mathcal W\) contains its mathematical witness. Equation (76.16) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS as a data schema.
