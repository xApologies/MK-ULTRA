# VOLUME IX - UNIVERSAL COUPLING

## Chapter 75 - Inputs, Domains, and Admissibility

### 75.1 A map begins with its domain

A mathematical operation is specified by more than a formula,

$$
F:X\longrightarrow Y,
\qquad
x\longmapsto F(x). \tag{75.1}
$$

Equation (75.1) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The same symbolic expression can define different objects on different domains or codomains.

### 75.2 Admissible input subset

If only some elements of \(X\) satisfy the theory’s requirements, define

$$
X_{\mathrm{adm}}
=\{x\in X:C_a(x)=0,\ I_b(x)\ge0\}. \tag{75.2}
$$

Equation (75.2) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Equality constraints and inequality constraints must be recorded separately and checked before applying a coupling map.

### 75.3 Partial maps

A map undefined on part of \(X\) is properly written

$$
F:X\rightharpoonup Y,
\qquad
\operatorname{dom}(F)\subseteq X. \tag{75.3}
$$

Equation (75.3) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Writing a total arrow where only a partial construction exists hides a substantive admissibility condition.

### 75.4 Regularity domain

For a differential operator of order \(k\), a typical domain is a Sobolev space,

$$
D(L)\subseteq H^k(\Omega),
\qquad
L:D(L)\longrightarrow L^2(\Omega). \tag{75.4}
$$

Equation (75.4) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Pointwise formulas do not guarantee that derivatives, boundary traces, or integrals exist.

### 75.5 Boundary conditions

For a boundary operator \(B\), the admissible operator domain may be

$$
D_B(L)
=\{u\in H^k(\Omega):Bu|_{\partial\Omega}=0\}. \tag{75.5}
$$

Equation (75.5) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Boundary conditions are part of the operator definition and can change its spectrum, kernel, and evolution.

### 75.6 Initial data constraints

For an evolution equation \(\partial_tu=\mathcal E[u]\), initial data must lie in a declared constraint surface,

$$
u(0)=u_0\in\mathcal C,
\qquad
\mathcal C=\{u:C[u]=0\}. \tag{75.6}
$$

Equation (75.6) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. A consistent evolution must also preserve \(\mathcal C\).

### 75.7 Constraint propagation

If

$$
\frac{d}{dt}C[u(t)]
=A[u(t)],C[u(t)], \tag{75.7}
$$

then \(C[u(0)]=0\) implies \(C[u(t)]=0\) where uniqueness holds. Equation (75.7) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS as a constraint-propagation schema.

### 75.8 Dimensional admissibility

A proposed relation

$$
Q=F(Q_1,\ldots,Q_n) \tag{75.8}
$$

is dimensionally admissible only if every additive term has the same dimension as \(Q\). Equation (75.8) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS as a unit-consistency requirement.

### 75.9 Product inputs

For a coupling of two sectors,

$$
\mathcal C:X_{\mathrm{adm}}\times Y_{\mathrm{adm}}
\longrightarrow Z, \tag{75.9}
$$

individual admissibility may be insufficient: compatibility constraints can select a smaller subset of the product. Equation (75.9) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS.

### 75.10 Compatibility locus

The compatible pairs are

$$
\Gamma_{XY}
=\{(x,y)\in X_{\mathrm{adm}}\times Y_{\mathrm{adm}}:
K(x,y)=0\}. \tag{75.10}
$$

Equation (75.10) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The true coupling domain is \(\Gamma_{XY}\), not automatically the full Cartesian product.

### 75.11 Symmetry reduction

If a group \(G\) acts on \(X_{\mathrm{adm}}\), physical inputs may be equivalence classes,

$$
\mathcal X_{\mathrm{phys}}
=X_{\mathrm{adm}}/G. \tag{75.11}
$$

Equation (75.11) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. A coupling must either be defined on the quotient or shown to be invariant along group orbits.

### 75.12 Gauge invariance of a map

For \(g\in G\), descent to the quotient requires

$$
F(g\cdot x)=F(x) \tag{75.12}
$$

or an explicitly covariant transformation in the codomain. Equation (75.12) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS.

### 75.13 Operator-domain discipline

For unbounded operators \(A\) and \(B\), the composition has domain

$$
D(AB)
=\{x\in D(B):Bx\in D(A)\}. \tag{75.13}
$$

Equation (75.13) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Symbolic juxtaposition does not prove that the composition exists on every state.

### 75.14 Self-adjoint admissibility

For quantum evolution generated by \(H\), one requires

$$
H=H^\dagger,
\qquad
D(H)=D(H^\dagger). \tag{75.14}
$$

Equation (75.14) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Formal symmetry on a test domain is not by itself self-adjointness.

### 75.15 Positivity and stability

An admissible energy functional may be required to satisfy

$$
E[x]\ge E_0
\qquad
\text{for all }x\in X_{\mathrm{adm}}. \tag{75.15}
$$

Equation (75.15) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Boundedness, local stability, and dynamical stability remain distinct tests.

### 75.16 Existence and image

Even when \(x\) is admissible, a target claim requires

$$
F(x)\in Y_{\mathrm{target}}
\subseteq Y. \tag{75.16}
$$

Equation (75.16) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. One must prove both existence of \(F(x)\) and membership in the asserted target class.

### 75.17 Typed admissibility contract

The disciplined sequence is

$$
X
\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{constraints}\ }
X_{\mathrm{adm}}
\xrightarrow{\ /G\ }
\mathcal X_{\mathrm{phys}}

Y_{\mathrm{target}}. \tag{75.17}
$$

Equation (75.17) is DETERMINISTIC_TYPE_NORMALIZATION. It separates raw inputs, constrained inputs, symmetry classes, the map, and its claimed image.
