# VOLUME XI - COSMOLOGY

## Chapter 92 - Cosmological Horizons and Dark Energy

### 92.1 Expansion scale

The background expansion rate is

$$
H(t)=\frac{\dot a(t)}{a(t)}. \tag{92.1}
$$

Equation (92.1) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The `@scale_factor` address is the most densely populated member of the assigned Library set.

### 92.2 Comoving Hubble scale

The comoving Hubble radius is

$$
r_H(t)=\frac{1}{a(t)H(t)}. \tag{92.2}
$$

Equation (92.2) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. It is a dynamical scale and is not generally identical to a causal particle or event horizon.

### 92.3 Particle horizon

The comoving particle-horizon distance is

$$
\chi_{\mathrm p}(t)=\int_{t_{\mathrm i}}^{t}\frac{dt'}{a(t')}. \tag{92.3}
$$

Equation (92.3) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Its existence and value depend on the past extent and behavior of the scale factor.

### 92.4 Event horizon

When the future integral converges, the comoving event horizon is

$$
\chi_{\mathrm e}(t)=\int_t^{t_{\mathrm f}}\frac{dt'}{a(t')}. \tag{92.4}
$$

Equation (92.4) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. It depends on the entire future evolution and therefore cannot be inferred from an instantaneous Hubble rate alone.

### 92.5 Proper horizon distances

The corresponding proper distances are

$$
D_{\mathrm p}(t)=a(t)\chi_{\mathrm p}(t),
\qquad
D_{\mathrm e}(t)=a(t)\chi_{\mathrm e}(t). \tag{92.5}
$$

Equation (92.5) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS and keeps comoving and physical distances distinct.

### 92.6 Apparent horizon

For FLRW geometry, the areal radius of the apparent horizon is

$$
R_A=\frac{1}{\sqrt{H^2+k/a^2}}. \tag{92.6}
$$

Equation (92.6) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Apparent, particle, event, and Hubble horizons are different constructions.

### 92.7 Background constraint

The expansion obeys

$$
H^2+\frac{k}{a^2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho+\frac{\Lambda}{3}. \tag{92.7}
$$

Equation (92.7) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. A dark-energy interpretation requires a source model or cosmological constant rather than an unexplained acceleration label.

### 92.8 Vacuum sector

A cosmological constant can be written as

$$
\rho_\Lambda=\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G},
\qquad
p_\Lambda=-\rho_\Lambda. \tag{92.8}
$$

Equation (92.8) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. It has equation-of-state parameter minus one and constant density.

### 92.9 Dark-energy conservation

For a separately conserved homogeneous component,

$$
\dot\rho_{\mathrm{de}}+3H
(\rho_{\mathrm{de}}+p_{\mathrm{de}})=0. \tag{92.9}
$$

Equation (92.9) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Interacting models require a nonzero exchange term and a specified partner sector.

### 92.10 Equation of state

The effective dark-energy parameter is

$$
w_{\mathrm{de}}(a)=\frac{p_{\mathrm{de}}(a)}{\rho_{\mathrm{de}}(a)}. \tag{92.10}
$$

Equation (92.10) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. A measured function w does not by itself identify microscopic origin.

### 92.11 Acceleration condition

Cosmic acceleration requires

$$
\rho_{\mathrm{tot}}+3p_{\mathrm{tot}}<0. \tag{92.11}
$$

Equation (92.11) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS in Einstein-FLRW cosmology. Modified gravity changes the interpretation of the effective source.

### 92.12 Time-varying parameterization

A common phenomenological form is

$$
w(a)=w_0+w_a(1-a). \tag{92.12}
$$

Equation (92.12) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS as a fit parameterization, not a fundamental equation.

### 92.13 Scalar-field example

For a canonical homogeneous scalar field,

$$
\rho_\phi=\frac{1}{2}\dot\phi^2+V(\phi),
\qquad
p_\phi=\frac{1}{2}\dot\phi^2-V(\phi). \tag{92.13}
$$

Equation (92.13) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The potential and initial data determine whether acceleration occurs.

### 92.14 Horizon thermodynamics

For an exact de Sitter horizon with radius H inverse,

$$
T_{\mathrm{dS}}=\frac{H}{2\pi},
\qquad
S_{\mathrm{dS}}=\frac{\pi}{G H^2}. \tag{92.14}
$$

Equation (92.14) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS in natural units. It does not establish a white-state infinity measure.

### 92.15 Distance observable

For spatially flat geometry, luminosity distance is

$$
d_L(z)=(1+z)\int_0^z\frac{dz'}{H(z')}. \tag{92.15}
$$

Equation (92.15) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Comparing dark-energy models requires a calibrated expansion history and observational likelihood.
