# VOLUME XI - COSMOLOGY

## Chapter 91 - Structure Formation

### 91.1 Perturbed cosmological fields

Matter density is decomposed into a homogeneous background and perturbation,

$$
\rho(t,\mathbf x)=\bar\rho(t)\,[1+\delta(t,\mathbf x)]. \tag{91.1}
$$

Equation (91.1) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The density contrast delta is the conventional starting point for linear structure formation.

### 91.2 Fourier modes

Spatial perturbations may be expanded as

$$
\delta(t,\mathbf x)=\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}
\,\delta_{\mathbf k}(t)e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}. \tag{91.2}
$$

Equation (91.2) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Translation invariance diagonalizes the linear evolution by wave vector.

### 91.3 Linear continuity equation

For a pressureless component in the Newtonian subhorizon regime,

$$
\dot\delta+\frac{1}{a}\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0. \tag{91.3}
$$

Equation (91.3) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS under the stated approximation.

### 91.4 Linear Euler equation

The peculiar velocity obeys

$$
\dot{\mathbf v}+H\mathbf v=-\frac{1}{a}\nabla\Phi. \tag{91.4}
$$

Equation (91.4) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Pressure, anisotropic stress, or native forces require additional terms.

### 91.5 Poisson equation

The Newtonian potential is related to the density perturbation by

$$
\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G a^2\bar\rho\,\delta. \tag{91.5}
$$

Equation (91.5) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS for general relativity in the appropriate limit.

### 91.6 Linear growth equation

Combining the preceding relations gives

$$
\ddot\delta+2H\dot\delta-4\pi G\bar\rho\,\delta=0. \tag{91.6}
$$

Equation (91.6) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. A native modification must identify whether it changes friction, gravitational response, or source clustering.

### 91.7 Growth factor

The growing solution is parameterized by

$$
\delta_{\mathbf k}(a)=D(a)\,\delta_{\mathbf k}(a_{\mathrm i}). \tag{91.7}
$$

Equation (91.7) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS when growth is scale-independent. Modified sectors may require D of both a and k.

### 91.8 Power spectrum

Statistical homogeneity defines

$$
\langle\delta(\mathbf k)\delta(\mathbf k')\rangle
=(2\pi)^3\delta_D(\mathbf k+\mathbf k')P_\delta(k). \tag{91.8}
$$

Equation (91.8) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. A theory becomes predictive only after specifying the initial spectrum and its transfer through cosmic history.

### 91.9 Transfer function

Linear processing of primordial modes is written

$$
P_\delta(k,a)=D(a)^2T(k)^2P_{\mathrm{prim}}(k). \tag{91.9}
$$

Equation (91.9) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Native phase or ER effects must enter through a derived background, transfer function, or source term.

### 91.10 Smoothed variance

On comoving scale R,

$$
\sigma_R^2=\int_0^\infty\frac{dk}{2\pi^2}
\,k^2P_\delta(k)\,|W(kR)|^2. \tag{91.10}
$$

Equation (91.10) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS and connects a spectrum to collapse statistics.

### 91.11 Collapse threshold

In spherical-collapse modeling, a region collapses when its linearly extrapolated contrast reaches

$$
\delta_L(a_{\mathrm{coll}})=\delta_c. \tag{91.11}
$$

Equation (91.11) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The value of delta-c is model- and background-dependent and must not be confused with the native `@collapse_limit` address.

### 91.12 Halo abundance schema

A conventional mass-function form is

$$
\frac{dn}{dM}=\frac{\bar\rho}{M}
f(\nu)\left|\frac{d\ln\sigma^{-1}}{dM}\right|,
\qquad \nu=\frac{\delta_c}{\sigma(M)}. \tag{91.12}
$$

Equation (91.12) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS as a statistical framework. The multiplicity function requires calibration or derivation.

### 91.13 Galactic rotation

For circular motion in an axisymmetric potential,

$$
v_c^2(r)=r\,\frac{\partial\Phi(r,0)}{\partial r}. \tag{91.13}
$$

Equation (91.13) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The address `@er_connectivity_effects_in_galactic_rotation` supplies a target relation but no assigned equation deriving an ER correction to the potential.

### 91.14 Spiral decomposition

A surface-density pattern may be decomposed as

$$
\Sigma(r,\phi,t)=\Sigma_0(r)
+\sum_{m\ge1}\Sigma_m(r,t)e^{im\phi}. \tag{91.14}
$$

Equation (91.14) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. A native phase-topology claim must provide mode dynamics, pattern speed, and stability conditions.
