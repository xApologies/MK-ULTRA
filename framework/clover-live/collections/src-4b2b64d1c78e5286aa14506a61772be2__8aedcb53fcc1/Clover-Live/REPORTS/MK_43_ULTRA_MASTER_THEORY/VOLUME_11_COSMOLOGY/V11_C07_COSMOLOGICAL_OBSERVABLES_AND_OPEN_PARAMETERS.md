### 93.1 Expansion history

Redshift and scale factor are related by

$$
1+z=\frac{a_0}{a},
\qquad
H(z)=\frac{\dot a}{a}. \tag{93.1}
$$

### 93.2 Radial distance

For a spatially flat background, the line-of-sight comoving distance is

$$
\chi(z)=\int_0^z\frac{dz'}{H(z')}. \tag{93.2}
$$

Equation (93.2) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS in units with light speed one.

### 93.3 Luminosity distance

The luminosity distance is

$$
d_L(z)=(1+z)\chi(z). \tag{93.3}
$$

Equation (93.3) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS for flat geometry and connects the expansion history to standard-candle observations.

### 93.4 Angular-diameter distance

The angular-diameter distance is

$$
d_A(z)=\frac{\chi(z)}{1+z}. \tag{93.4}
$$

Equation (93.4) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS for flat geometry and enters standard-ruler measurements.

### 93.5 Distance modulus

Observed magnitudes are commonly expressed through

$$

Equation (93.5) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Absolute calibration and selection effects remain part of the likelihood.

### 93.6 Isotropic BAO distance

A commonly used combined distance is

$$
D_V(z)=\left[(1+z)^2d_A(z)^2\frac{z}{H(z)}\right]^{1/3}. \tag{93.6}
$$

Equation (93.6) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS in natural units. Modern analyses may retain transverse and radial information separately.
