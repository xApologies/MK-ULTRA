# VOLUME VII - GRAVITY

## Chapter 63 - Gravitational Waves and Radiation

### 63.1 Radiative perturbations

In the weak-field regime, write

$$
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu},
\qquad
|h_{\mu\nu}|\ll1. \tag{63.1}
$$

Equation (63.1) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. A gravitational wave is a propagating, radiative sector of the metric perturbation, not every perturbation and not the whole gravitational field.

### 63.2 Vacuum wave equation

With trace reversal and harmonic gauge, the vacuum linearized Einstein equation is

$$
\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu}=0,
\qquad
\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu}=0. \tag{63.2}
$$

Equation (63.2) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. Its derivation assumes a flat background, weak perturbations, and vanishing local stress-energy. None of those conditions may be dropped silently.

### 63.3 Plane-wave solutions

A complex plane-wave representative has the form

$$
\bar h_{\mu\nu}(x)
=\operatorname{Re}\!\left(
A_{\mu\nu}e^{ik_\rho x^\rho}
\right),
\qquad
k^\rho k_\rho=0,
\qquad
k^\mu A_{\mu\nu}=0. \tag{63.3}
$$

Equation (63.3) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The null dispersion relation and transversality condition follow from Equation (63.2); they are not independent postulates.

### 63.4 Transverse-traceless gauge

For a wave propagating through a suitable vacuum region, the transverse-traceless conditions are

$$
h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{0\mu}=0,
\qquad
\partial^i h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}=0,
\qquad
\delta^{ij}h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}=0. \tag{63.4}
$$

Equation (63.4) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. TT gauge isolates the two radiative degrees of freedom in general relativity. It is a gauge specialization, not a definition of every candidate MK-43 wave mode.

### 63.5 Polarization decomposition

For propagation along a chosen direction,

$$
h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}
=h_{+}\,e^{+}_{ij}
+h_{\times}\,e^{\times}_{ij}. \tag{63.5}
$$

Equation (63.5) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The plus and cross tensors depend on a transverse basis. Their basis rotation does not create new physical polarizations.

### 63.6 Local tidal action

The measurable separation \(\xi^i\) of nearby freely falling bodies obeys

$$
\frac{d^2\xi^i}{dt^2}
=-R^{i}{}_{0j0}\xi^j
\simeq
\frac12\ddot h^{\mathrm{TT}\,i}{}_{j}\xi^j. \tag{63.6}
$$

Equation (63.6) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS in the local detector frame and the stated convention. It links wave curvature to an observable differential acceleration without equating coordinate displacement with a physical strain.

### 63.7 Strain response

For an interferometer arm directed along the unit vector \(n^i\),

$$
\frac{\delta L}{L}
=\frac12 n^i n^j h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}. \tag{63.7}
$$

Equation (63.7) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS in the long-wavelength idealization. A complete detector model also includes light propagation, antenna response, calibration, and noise.

### 63.8 Effective wave stress-energy

At wavelengths short compared with the background-curvature scale, an averaged effective tensor is

$$
t^{\mathrm{GW}}_{\mu\nu}
=\frac{c^4}{32\pi G}
\left\langle
\nabla_\mu h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}
\nabla_\nu h_{\mathrm{TT}}^{ij}
\right\rangle. \tag{63.8}
$$

Equation (63.8) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS in the Isaacson-type high-frequency regime. Gravitational radiation carries energy and momentum only through an averaged, approximation-dependent construction; it is not represented by an ordinary local tensor at every scale.

### 63.9 Energy flux

For an outgoing wave in the radiation zone,

$$
\mathcal F
=\frac{c^3}{32\pi G}
\left\langle
\dot h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}
\dot h_{\mathrm{TT}}^{ij}
\right\rangle. \tag{63.9}
$$

Equation (63.9) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. @gravitational_radiation must remain distinct from @gravitational_wave: the first emphasizes transported energy-momentum, while the second names the propagating geometric disturbance.

### 63.10 Quadrupole emission

For a slow-motion, weak-field source, the leading radiated power is

$$
P
=\frac{G}{5c^5}
\left\langle
\dddot Q_{ij}\dddot Q^{ij}
\right\rangle. \tag{63.10}
$$

Equation (63.10) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. The trace-free mass quadrupole \(Q_{ij}\) and the slow-motion approximation are essential. The formula is not a universal strong-field emission law.

### 63.11 Curved-background propagation

On a vacuum curved background, a Lorenz-gauge perturbation satisfies a Lichnerowicz-type equation,

$$
\bar\nabla^\rho\bar\nabla_\rho h_{\mu\nu}
+2\bar R_{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}h^{\rho\sigma}
=0. \tag{63.11}
$$

Equation (63.11) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS under the stated vacuum and gauge assumptions. Background curvature scatters and focuses waves, so propagation is not generally identical to flat-space translation.

### 63.12 Geometric-optics limit

For \(h_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{Re}(A_{\mu\nu}e^{i\theta/\epsilon})\), the leading wave covector \(k_\mu=\nabla_\mu\theta\) obeys

$$
k^\mu k_\mu=0,
\qquad
k^\nu\nabla_\nu k^\mu=0. \tag{63.12}
$$

Equation (63.12) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS in the geometric-optics limit. Wavefronts follow null geodesics at leading order, while amplitude and polarization transport enter at subsequent orders.

### 63.13 Far-zone decay

For an isolated source in four spacetime dimensions, the leading radiative amplitude scales as

$$
h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}(t,r)
=\frac{1}{r}
H^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}\!\left(t-\frac{r}{c}\right)
+O(r^{-2}). \tag{63.13}
$$

Equation (63.13) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS in an asymptotically flat radiation zone. It separates retarded propagation and geometric dilution from source-specific waveform structure.

### 63.14 Wave memory

A permanent radiative displacement is encoded by

$$
\Delta h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}
=\lim_{u\to+\infty}h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}(u)
-\lim_{u\to-\infty}h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}(u). \tag{63.14}
$$

Equation (63.14) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS as the asymptotic definition of memory. Whether a native MK-43 mechanism predicts an additional memory component requires a sourced calculation, not an address-space association.

### 63.15 Lensing is a distinct observable

For a weak, isolated point mass, the leading light-deflection angle is

$$
\alpha\simeq\frac{4GM}{bc^2}. \tag{63.15}
$$

Equation (63.15) is EXTERNAL_ESTABLISHED_MATHEMATICS. @gravitational_lensing belongs in the gravity neighborhood but is not a gravitational wave. Lensing probes the geometry traversed by null rays; radiation probes propagating changes in that geometry.
